# Getting the First 25 Meaningful Users — The Seat

*Written June 2026. Perspective: VP Marketing.*
*Objective: 25 users who activate, give feedback, and help validate PMF. Not vanity metrics — real conversations.*

---

## What "Meaningful" Means

A meaningful user is not someone who signed up and never came back. A meaningful user:
- Completed at least one full session (upload → scan → chat → punch list)
- Either gave feedback or is willing to get on a call
- Represents a real buyer persona (instructional designer, facilitator, or learning leader)

We want 25 of these. We probably need to reach 150–200 people to get there.

---

## The Three Buyer Personas

**1. The Quality-Obsessed Instructional Designer**
Senior IC. Has been in bad review meetings. Cares deeply about craft. Already doing informal self-review. Will adopt individually. Budget is personal or expensed.

*Where to find them:* LinkedIn, r/instructionaldesign, ID-specific Slack groups, ATD communities, Articulate's community forums.

*What they need to hear:* "Find out what your skeptic will say before your review does."

**2. The Standard-Setting Learning Leader**
Head of L&D, VP Talent Development, CLO. Manages a team of designers. Review cycles are a cost center. Has budget. Buys for the team.

*Where to find them:* LinkedIn (search: "Head of L&D," "VP Learning," "Director of Talent Development"), ATD Chapter leaders, Learning Guild conference attendees.

*What they need to hear:* "Send this to your team before the next review cycle. Stop having the same conversations."

**3. The Facilitator-Designer Hybrid**
Does both: designs the program AND delivers it. Feels the gap between "how I designed this" and "how people actually experienced it" viscerally. Highly self-critical. Will use The Seat obsessively if it resonates.

*Where to find them:* LinkedIn, ATD, facilitation-specific communities (Liberating Structures, facilitation Slack groups).

*What they need to hear:* "See your training through the eyes of the people you're designing for."

## Outreach Strategy

### Principle: Quality Over Quantity

Do not blast 500 DMs. Send 25 thoughtful messages and get 15 replies. The goal is conversations, not clicks.

Each outreach message should:
- Reference something specific about the person (a post they wrote, a job they hold, a company you recognize)
- Name the problem in one sentence without explaining the solution
- Ask for 20 minutes, not a demo

**Template (DM / Email):**

> Hi [name] — I saw your post about [L&D topic / training review / design craft]. It landed.
>
> I've been building something for exactly the problem you're describing: that moment in a review meeting when someone flags something you could have caught three weeks ago. I'd love to get your reaction to it — not a sales call, just a founder trying to learn from people who actually do this work.
>
> Would 20 minutes make sense this week?

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't explain the product in the first message
- Don't send a Calendly link in the first message
- Don't use "I wanted to reach out" or "I hope this finds you well"
- Don't send the same message to 50 people

## LinkedIn Strategy

LinkedIn is the primary channel. L&D practitioners live there. They post about their work, their frustrations, and their craft more than almost any other professional community.

### Profile Setup (if not already done)
Julie's LinkedIn profile should clearly position The Seat:
- Headline: "Founder, The Seat | L&D pressure-testing tool for training designers"
- About: The problem in two sentences. The persona names. One testimonial quote.
- Featured: Link to theseatmethod.com and the "Before You Take a Seat" checklist

### Content Strategy (3 posts per week for 6 weeks)

The goal of content is not to go viral. It is to appear credible and authentic to the 200 people who matter.

**Post types that work for this audience:**

**Type 1: The Honest Problem Post**
Share a real story about a bad review meeting — not as a pitch for The Seat, just as a recognition of a shared experience. "We've all been in that room." Get 50 IDs nodding along. The Seat gets mentioned in the comments, not the post.

*Example:* "The most expensive training meeting I've ever sat in lasted 90 minutes. The feedback that caused the most revision was something two people could have caught in a 10-minute desk review three weeks earlier. We've all been in this meeting. I built something for it."

**Type 2: The Insight Post**
Share something useful from The Seat's research or Julie's expertise — not a product pitch. Dana's most common question. What Marcus needs in the first 30 seconds. Why Bex's enthusiasm is a trap.

*Example:* "The Skeptic in your next training session isn't hostile. She's withholding. There's a difference. Here's what she's waiting for before she leans in."

**Type 3: The Artifact Post**
Share a real punch list (anonymized) with permission. "This is what The Seat said about [type of training]. See if you recognize the feedback." Let the output speak for itself.

**Type 4: The Customer Story**
Post the testimonial quotes — one at a time, spaced out. "An Analytics Leader at a global media company said this after their first session. We built this for exactly this moment." Tag the user if they're comfortable.

**Type 5: The Direct Ask**
Every few weeks: "I'm looking for 5 L&D designers to pressure-test something I'm building. 20-minute call. Free access in exchange for honest feedback. Reply or DM if you're interested." These convert.

### Engagement Strategy

Comment authentically on 5–10 L&D posts per day. Not "great post!" — real responses that add to the conversation. This surfaces Julie's name to the right people without a single dollar of advertising.

Target accounts to engage with: L&D influencers, ATD chapter accounts, Articulate, Learning Guild, 360Learning, Docebo (their community teams post regularly). Also: individual practitioners with 2,000–20,000 followers who are clearly respected in the community. These are the connectors.

## Founder-Led Marketing Ideas

These are high-signal, low-scale tactics that fit where Julie is right now.

**1. The "Live Seat" Demo**
Host a 30-minute Zoom session — open to any L&D practitioner — where Julie takes a volunteer's real training content and runs it through The Seat live. No polish. Just real feedback from real personas on real work. Record it. Share the clip. Do one per month.

**2. The "Before Your Review" Email**
Identify 10 people in Julie's network who have a training review coming up in the next 2–4 weeks. Email them directly: "I know you have [project] coming up. Want to run it through The Seat before the review? I'll give you a code and be available if anything breaks." This is a warm, high-conversion offer.

**3. The LinkedIn Comment-to-DM Funnel**
Find posts where L&D practitioners are venting about review meetings, design feedback, or training quality. Leave a substantive comment. If they engage, follow up in DM with "This is literally what I built something for. Interested in a look?"

**4. The ATD Chapter Talk**
Pitch a 20-minute virtual talk to 3 ATD chapters: "What I Learned from Having 3 AI Learners Review My Training." Tell the story. The Seat is the vehicle. Most chapters are desperate for content. Offer to provide real value, not a product demo.

**5. The "Before You Take a Seat" Checklist Distribution**
Post the checklist to every community above. No pitch. Just: "Made this for L&D teams who want to catch problems before review. Free download, no email required." Watch where the referrals come from. The checklist is the best top-of-funnel asset we have.

**6. The Podcast Circuit**
There are 15–20 L&D podcasts with 5,000–50,000 listeners that would take a founder interview. The pitch: "Founder of The Seat talking about what 3 AI learner personas reveal about L&D review culture." This is a 6–8 week lead time but reaches exactly the right audience at scale.

## Customer Interview Plan

Before the 25th user is activated, Julie should have completed 10 interviews. Here is the plan.

### Who to Interview
- The 3 testimonial-givers (first priority — they've already shown enthusiasm)
- 3–5 beta users who activated (ran a full session)
- 2–3 people who signed up but never activated (what stopped them?)
- 2–3 L&D leaders who haven't tried it but have been in a review meeting recently

### How to Get the Meeting
Short DM or email:
> "You used The Seat recently — I'd love 20 minutes of your honest reaction. No sales agenda. I'm genuinely trying to learn. Can we find a time this week?"

Target 80% yes rate from people who've completed a session. They want to help.

### The 7 Questions (Ask in Order, Don't Deviate)

1. **Tell me about the last training review that didn't go the way you hoped.** *(Don't mention The Seat. Let them tell the story.)*

2. **What did you upload to The Seat? What were you hoping to learn?** *(Understand the use case they brought.)*

3. **Walk me through the session. What surprised you?** *(Let them narrate. Listen for emotion.)*

4. **What was the most useful part?** *(The answer will tell you what to double down on.)*

5. **What confused you or almost made you stop?** *(The answer will tell you what to fix.)*

6. **Did you do anything differently after the session — in the training or in how you thought about the review?** *(This is the outcome question. Behavior change = real value.)*

7. **If The Seat cost $X/month, would you pay for it? What about $Y?** *(Ask two price points. Watch the reaction before they answer.)*

### What to Do With the Answers
- After each call: write 3 sentences summarizing the most important thing you learned
- Update CUSTOMER_LEARNINGS.md within 24 hours
- Look for the pattern that appears in 3+ interviews — that's signal

## What This Is Not

- A launch campaign
- A PR blitz
- A paid ads strategy
- A referral program
- A product hunt launch

All of those come later. Right now the job is to find 25 people who will have real conversations with you about a real problem. That work is done in DMs, on calls, and in community posts — not in ad dashboards.

The goal of the first 25 users is not revenue (though revenue would be nice). The goal is 10 completed customer interviews that tell you with confidence: who uses this, why, what they'd pay, and whether they come back.

Everything else is built on that foundation.
