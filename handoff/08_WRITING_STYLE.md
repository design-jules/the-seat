# Writing Style — The Seat

*For any agent writing copy, UI text, persona chat, emails, or marketing content for The Seat.*

---

## The Voice

**Warm, direct, a little irreverent. Not corporate.**

Julie Kirkham built this from her own experience as an L&D designer. Every piece of copy should sound like it came from someone who has sat in those bad review meetings, not someone who read about them.

---

## Hard Rules (Non-Negotiable)

### No em-dashes
Never use — this. Use a comma, period, or restructure the sentence instead.

**Wrong:** "Dana — The Skeptic — will give you the feedback your VP will."
**Right:** "Dana, The Skeptic, will give you the feedback your VP will."

### No asterisk stage directions
Never use `*leans back*`, `*sighs*`, `*nods slowly*`, or any other stage-direction style action in persona chat or marketing copy.

### No periods after page headlines
Headlines end cleanly. No period at the end.

**Wrong:** "Design from the seat that matters."
**Right:** "Design from the seat that matters"

### Don't start a sentence with "Like,"
"Like, did it actually sound like a real learner?" is not how Julie writes.

### Don't start a sentence with "I wanted to reach out" or "I hope this finds you well"
These are the hallmarks of mass-blasted email. The Seat's outreach is personal and direct.

---

## Tone Patterns

### What it sounds like
- Short sentences. Confident. Doesn't over-explain.
- Refers to real, specific moments (the review meeting, the feeling of walking in with work you're proud of)
- Trusts the reader to get it
- Not trying to impress — trying to connect

### What it doesn't sound like
- Corporate L&D speak ("stakeholder alignment," "learner journey optimization")
- AI-generated enthusiasm ("I'm so excited to share," "This game-changing tool")
- Constructed emotion that reads fake ("The kind where you're like, oh.")
- Long windup before the point

---

## Persona Copy Rules

The three personas (Dana, Marcus, Bex) each have a distinct voice. Across all three:
- Reference actual content from the training — don't speak generically
- Don't use stage directions (`*settles in*`)
- Don't use the persona's name in every message — only when it feels natural
- Dana: dry, withholding, specific. Earns warmth slowly.
- Marcus: practical, slightly distracted, cuts to utility
- Bex: enthusiastic, engaged, doesn't flag real problems

---

## Marketing / Outreach Patterns

**For DMs and personal outreach:**
- Reference something specific about the person first
- Name the problem in one sentence
- Ask for 20 minutes, not a demo
- No Calendly link in the first message

**For LinkedIn posts:**
- Lead with the story or the insight, not the product
- Mention The Seat in context, not as a pitch
- Conversational, not broadcast

**For beta invites (Julie's actual voice):**
- Chosen/trusted feel — not a mass blast
- "You're someone I trust" not "We're excited to offer you"
- Specific reason you're inviting this person

## Words to Avoid

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Stakeholder | Reviewer, the room, whoever's reviewing |
| Learner journey | How people actually experience training |
| Bite-sized | Short, focused |
| Leverage | Use |
| Robust | Strong, solid |
| Seamless | Smooth, easy |
| Utilize | Use |
| Game-changing | (just don't) |
| Cutting-edge | (just don't) |
| Excited to share | (just don't) |

---

## Example: Good vs. Not Good

**Not good (too AI):**
"The Seat is a cutting-edge, AI-powered solution that leverages advanced language models to provide L&D professionals with actionable insights — delivering a seamless pre-review experience that drives learner outcomes."

**Good:**
"You upload your training. Three very different learners read it and tell you what they actually think. You get a list of what to fix before anyone else sees it."

---

## Referencing the Personas in Copy

Always use their full names on first reference: Dana, The Skeptic / Marcus, The Slammed / Bex, The Hype.

Note the comma between name and archetype — not an em-dash.

After first reference, first name alone is fine: "what Dana will say," "Marcus's kind of training," "when Bex loves everything."
